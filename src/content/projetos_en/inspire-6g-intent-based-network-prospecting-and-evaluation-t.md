## Project Overview

This research project aims to develop a tool for evaluating and optimizing Intent-Based Networks (IBN) in the context of 6G networks. With rapid technological advancement in the area of ​​electronic components and telecommunications, network softwarization has become fundamental for the next generation of telecommunications networks, such as 5G and 6G. IBN networks, which use Machine Learning and Artificial Intelligence techniques, have the potential to bring flexibility, agility and simplification to network infrastructure. However, we face significant challenges in creating and managing IBN networks. One of the challenges identified is the lack of a set of tools for evaluating these networks. In this project, we propose the development of an IBN framework that will allow the construction and evaluation of IBN network scenarios. The framework will be integrated with the Mininet1 network emulator, widely used by the academic network community, providing a flexible and realistic environment for tests and simulations. Another challenge addressed in this project is conflict resolution and efficient resource allocation in IBN networks. We intend to explore heuristics based on Artificial Intelligence to solve these complex problems. By considering conflict resolution and resource allocation jointly, it is expected to achieve better allocation and utilization rates of network resources. The project will follow a methodology that includes literature review, definition of framework requirements, design and implementation of the framework, in addition to testing and performance evaluation. Experiments will be carried out using different IBN network scenarios to validate the effectiveness and efficiency of the proposed tool, as well as comparison with other potential similar solutions, when existing in the literature. The result of this project is expected to contribute to the advancement of IBN networks in the context of 6G, providing an open source, practical and flexible tool for evaluating, optimizing and simulating these networks. Furthermore, the joint approach to conflict resolution and resource allocation can make valuable contributions to improving performance and utilization of network resources. Finally, the development of this project will generate several positive impacts both for the academic community of the Federal District and for the international community, given the planned collaboration with the University of Braunschweig, Germany.
