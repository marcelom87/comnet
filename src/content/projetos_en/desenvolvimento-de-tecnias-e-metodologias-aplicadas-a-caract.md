## Project Overview

The last decade has been marked by the increasing use of wireless networking technologies for last-mile broadband Internet access. However, due to their distributed nature, wireless networks present many challenges when faced with the growing demand for multimedia applications, which require more rigorous control of available resources. The increase in the number of wireless network users, associated with a growing demand for greater speed and timing restrictions on the part of applications, requires mechanisms that allow analyzing and improving the performance of the layers involved in the transmission process. One of the topics that has been researched in relation to the area of ​​network traffic modeling is the flow of a communication channel. By measuring the effective capacity of a communication channel, it is possible to obtain results that guide network administrators in modeling their users' profiles. Better knowledge of the bandwidth limit available per user will allow for better sizing of resources and better provision of Quality of Service (QoS) for applications. This research project aims to contribute to the characterization process and methodologies for traffic sizing. It is expected to advance issues related to the calculation of QoS metrics, traffic policing, traffic aggregation, and collaborative computing that can be explored through understanding the characteristics intrinsic to the application profile.
