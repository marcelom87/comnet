## Project Overview

Currently in computing, one of the devices that has been gaining popularity in the prototyping of digital circuits and systems are FPGAs (Field Programmable Gate Arryas). FPGAs are composed of thousands of simple logical elements (Logic Elements) that can be interconnected (configured) to form complex digital systems. At the same time, another way of solving problems, called Genetic Programming, based on genetic algorithms, has been used for a long time for software-related problems. Genetic algorithms rely heavily on the principles of natural selection for problem solving [1]. The individuals of a population represent the possible solutions and are encoded in a sequence of bits called bitstring, acting as chromosomes, describing this solution. New populations are formed and tested using a fitness function, in which each individual is evaluated and associated with a score that indicates their fitness, that is, how close the individual is to the desired result. This score determines their chances of reproducing and passing on their chromosomes to subsequent populations [3]. In this way, as new iterations (generations) are made, poorly adapted individuals tend not to survive and the most adapted ones tend to reproduce. Over time, due to the crossover and mutation mechanisms generally found in such algorithms, small modifications can be observed, resulting in new solutions and possibly also generating an increase in the fitness of the population and, consequently, a closer approximation to the appropriate solution. The main motivation of this work was the union of the two fields presented and possibly the exploration of a solution space that has not yet been well explored. The use of both techniques can be applied to both digital and analog circuits [4]. In this work, only the application of genetic algorithms to digital circuits, both combinational and sequential, will be explored. Objectives: This work's main objective is to study and propose new techniques of intrinsic genetic evolution for the design of digital circuits in FPGA that have characteristics that surpass classical solutions, whether in terms of size (number of transistors and/or logic gates), which implies lower manufacturing costs; propagation delay, which provides greater frequency of use; and/or energy efficiency. With this proposition, we hope to contribute to the state of the art, explore and study projects that have not yet been achieved in the solutions space. Experiments with evolutionary designs of basic combinational circuits, such as adders, multiplexers, multipliers and decoders will be carried out, as well as experiments with sequential circuits (with memory), such as latches, flip-flops and state machines.
