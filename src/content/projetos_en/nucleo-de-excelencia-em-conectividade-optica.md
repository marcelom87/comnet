## Project Overview

The extraordinary development of photonics of the last decades has given it an outstanding role in the communication networks, especially in the transmission of large flows of aggregated information between network nodes. However, most nodal functions needed to assemble and route these flows around the network must still be realized by electronic means, due to current limitations of photonics. The need to combine the huge transmission capacity of optical fibers with the unmatched processing power of microelectronic circuits has generated radical changes in the architecture of communication networks. The continuing evolution of both photonics and electronics keeps this evolution from stabilizing, thus bringing uncertainty to the technological scenarios of the future. The uncertainties are highlighted when one considers the characteristics of the traffic to be flown by emerging networks. The traffic generated by the Internet already dominates modern networks, although their revenue still comes predominantly from traditional business, especially telephony. With its current spectrum of applications, Internet traffic is very bursty, even to the point of exhibiting fractal characteristics (self-similarity), thus requiring an intelligent management of resources (or, alternatively, their overdeployment) in order to guarantee or to promote the qualities-of- service required by the applications. Appropriate management of the network resources is also necessary to assure the survival of services under failures (e.g. fiber cuts) that may occur in communication networks. Supplying differentiated grades of protection and service restoration is also a timely proposition, considering the diversity of applications and, consequently, of the qualities- of-service to be supplied by such networks..
